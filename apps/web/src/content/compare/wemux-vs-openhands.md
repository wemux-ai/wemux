# Wemux vs OpenHands

OpenHands is widely recognized in the AI development space, but it plays a different role from wemux.

OpenHands is broader. It behaves more like an AI-driven development platform and agent infrastructure layer. Wemux is narrower on purpose: it focuses on the control surface for routed execution, delivery visibility, and real coding work across machines.

## Where OpenHands is strong

OpenHands has strength in platform breadth:

- broad development-platform positioning
- agent framework and extension surface
- cloud, local, and enterprise-facing narratives
- experimentation around delegation and general agent capability

That makes it attractive to teams or builders who want a larger AI development platform footprint rather than a tightly defined delivery workflow.

## Where Wemux is more specific

Wemux is more opinionated about the execution chain:

- route the task
- send it to the right worker
- run in the real repo and environment
- return logs, branches, and reviewable outcomes
- keep the workflow visible to humans

That is a more specific product contract than “general AI development platform.” It is closer to a delivery operating layer for AI coding.

## Side-by-side

| Dimension | OpenHands | Wemux |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product shape | Broad AI development platform | AI coding delivery control plane |
| Main strength | Platform breadth and ecosystem reach | Routed execution and visible delivery workflow |
| Best fit | Teams exploring a wider agent platform surface | Teams already dealing with real repo execution and operational handoff |
| Delivery closure | Broader platform story, less centered on task-board delivery control | More focused on execution, review context, and handoff continuity |
| Machine ownership | Can support execution surfaces, but less centered on worker routing as the product narrative | Worker routing is central to the product story |
| Human oversight | Depends on product layer and workflow setup | Human review and visible execution are core expectations |

## Which one to choose

Choose OpenHands if your priority is a broad AI development platform with a larger ecosystem and infrastructure footprint.

Choose Wemux if your priority is making AI coding work operational in real environments, where the problem is not only model capability but execution ownership, machine routing, and reviewable delivery.

## Why the distinction matters

Many teams do not fail because they lack another agent framework. They fail because work gets stuck between the model and the real repository:

- the right environment is on a specific machine
- nobody can see which task is running where
- outputs are hard to inspect as delivery artifacts
- context continuity breaks across sessions and devices

Wemux is aimed at that exact last-mile problem. So the comparison is not “which product is more generally powerful.” The comparison is “which product is more aligned with turning AI coding into accountable delivery work.”
