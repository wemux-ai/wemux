# What changes when teams move beyond demos

Wemux does not attack chat. It fills the gap between a promising answer and a traceable result that a team can inspect, merge, and operate.

## Where chat still works well

- Fast brainstorming, one-off coding help, and local reasoning.
- Explaining APIs, reviewing snippets, and prototyping ideas.
- Helping one person think better before work starts.

## Where delivery becomes the real problem

- Routing work into the right repository and runtime.
- Keeping logs, branch outputs, retries, and approvals visible.
- Coordinating multiple agents and multiple workers without chaos.

## Side-by-side

| Dimension | AI chat | AI delivery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Task flow | Great for one request, weak at long-running ownership. | Tasks stay visible across intake, execution, review, and retry. |
| Execution surface | Often tied to a browser tab or one machine context. | Routes work to the right local box, private machine, or cloud worker. |
| Result shape | Output is usually a message, snippet, or ad hoc patch. | Output includes branches, commits, logs, artifacts, and review notes. |
| Governance | Easy to lose why a change happened or who approved it. | Humans keep approval while the system records what happened. |

## The working model

Teams do not need to choose one forever. They need a cleaner contract between generation and delivery.

Use chat to think faster. Use a delivery console to route, execute, review, and ship real work.

That is the position behind Wemux: not another assistant tab, but a system for turning AI output into accountable team execution.
